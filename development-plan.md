# WildAlert Development Plan

**Status:** Concept and research stage; no working prototype is claimed.

## Objective
Explore whether a responsible, location-aware early-warning and decision-support tool can help reduce the risks associated with human–wildlife conflict in Botswana.

## Workstreams

### 1. User and partner discovery
**Activities**
- Interview community members, farmers, wildlife officers, and conservation partners.
- Document how wildlife sightings and incidents are currently reported.
- Identify who should receive which information and what actions are safe.

**Outputs**
- User needs summary
- Stakeholder map
- Prioritised use cases

### 2. Data feasibility and governance
**Activities**
- Identify possible incident, location, environmental, and sensor data sources.
- Assess data access, coverage, quality, update frequency, and permission requirements.
- Define retention, access control, consent, and location-privacy practices.

**Outputs**
- Data inventory
- Data quality assessment
- Draft data-management and privacy rules

### 3. Prototype scope
**Initial candidate workflow**
- Submit or import a sample incident report.
- Review and label reports as unverified, verified, or rejected.
- Display appropriate reports on a map.
- Provide a simple dashboard for reviewing patterns.

**Decision to make**
Start with transparent mapping or rule-based indicators unless sufficient reliable data and evaluation justify machine learning.

### 4. Evaluation and safety
**Measures to define**
- Report completeness and verification rate
- Time between report and review
- Usability for intended users
- False-alarm and missed-event rates, where measurable
- Whether alerts are understandable, actionable, and safe

**Safety gate**
Do not use a prototype to issue operational safety alerts until it has been tested and relevant local partners have agreed on procedures and responsibilities.

## Suggested milestones

1. Validate problem and users.
2. Confirm data access and safeguards.
3. Specify a small prototype.
4. Build a demonstration with clearly labelled sample or authorised data.
5. Test with advisors and intended users.
6. Decide whether a controlled pilot is justified.

Dates and owners should be added after the team, available resources, and partner commitments are confirmed.
